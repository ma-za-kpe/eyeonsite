## Tier 1: Direct Competitors (Africa-focused Verification)

### LandSafe (Nigeria)

**What they do:** Property verification service for the Nigerian market, combining vetted lawyers with AI technology. Delivers verification reports within 24–48 hours.

**Markets:** Abuja, Lagos, Ogun State (Nigeria only)

**Model:** Managed service. Client requests verification → LandSafe assigns internal team (lawyers + field agents) → Report delivered. Not a marketplace — LandSafe employs/contracts the verifiers.

**Strengths:**
- Legal expertise (lawyers verify title documents, not just physical presence)
- AI component for document analysis
- Established in Nigeria's most active real estate markets
- Positioned specifically for diaspora buyers

**Weaknesses:**
- Nigeria only — no multi-country play
- Managed service doesn't scale like a marketplace
- Likely high per-verification cost (legal team involvement)
- Not real-time or on-demand — 24-48hr turnaround
- No recurring/subscription model visible

**EyeOnSite positioning vs. LandSafe:**
- We're a marketplace (scalable) vs. their managed service (labor-intensive)
- We're multi-country (Accra first, then Lagos)
- We're on-demand (hours, not days)
- They're stronger on legal/title verification — we should partner, not compete, on legal aspects
- Co-exist: LandSafe for high-value legal title verification, EyeOnSite for ongoing construction monitoring

### Mantel Verify (Nigeria)

**What they do:** Premium independent verification for diaspora investors. Combines technology, legal professionals, and on-ground teams.

**Model:** High-touch premium service. Likely $100–$500+ per verification.

**Strengths:**
- Premium positioning (higher margins)
- Independent (not affiliated with developers/agents)
- Multiple verification types (physical, legal, financial)

**Weaknesses:**
- Premium = small market (only wealthy diaspora)
- Not scalable — dependent on professional network
- No visible platform/app
- Nigeria-focused

**EyeOnSite positioning vs. Mantel Verify:**
- We serve the mass market ($20-$50/task vs. $100-$500)
- We're technology-first, they're relationship-first
- They own the high-end; we own the volume

**Model:** Vertically integrated. Property verification is one feature of a broader diaspora real estate platform.

**Strengths:**

- Diaspora mortgage product (unique financial product)
- Full-stack approach (verification + financing)

**Weaknesses:**

- Nigeria only

**EyeOnSite positioning vs. LANDIZ:**

## Tier 2: Adjacent Players (US-focused Verification Marketplaces)

### WeGoLook (Crawford & Company)

**What they do:** On-demand field inspection platform with 45,000+ "Lookers" across US, Canada, and UK. Founded 2009 in Oklahoma City. Acquired by Crawford & Company in 2016. Raised $3.35M pre-acquisition.

**Markets:** US, Canada, UK

**Model:** Two-sided marketplace. Clients (insurance companies, law firms, lenders, auto dealerships) post "Looks." Lookers accept, travel to site, capture standardized evidence. Pay: $15–$75/task depending on complexity.

**Verticals:** Insurance claims (primary), property condition, auto verification, document pickup, court filing, asset verification.

**Technology:** Mobile app for Lookers. Web portal for clients. Standardized capture templates. Photo + checklist.

**Strengths:**
- 45K+ Lookers — massive supply network
- Acquired by Crawford (global claims management, $1.8B revenue)
- Proven model for 10+ years
- Enterprise client base (insurance companies)
- Standardized quality through templates

**Weaknesses:**
- US/Canada/UK only — no African presence
- Insurance-heavy (not diversified)
- Post-acquisition: likely less agile, more corporate
- Low Looker pay ($15-$75) — quality concerns at lower end
- No escrow (enterprise invoicing model)

**Lessons for EyeOnSite:**
- Template-driven inspections WORK at scale — our checklist approach is validated
- Insurance is a massive vertical — add to V3 enterprise offering
- 45K+ agents proves the supply side scales
- $3.35M raised before acquisition — lean capital can build this
- Acquisition exit path is real (Crawford paid for the network, not the tech)

---

### iVueit

**What they do:** Commercial property and facilities verification platform. 30,000+ "Vuers" inspect retail locations, commercial properties, and facilities.

**Markets:** US only

**Model:** Marketplace. Clients post "Vues" (verification tasks). Vuers accept and complete. Pay: $5–$20/task (low end).

**Strengths:**
- Large Vuer network (30K+)
- Commercial/retail focus (consistent, recurring tasks)
- Simple task model (drive by, take photos, answer questions)

**Weaknesses:**
- Very low per-task pay → quality concerns
- US only
- Commoditized (drive-by verification, minimal expertise needed)
- No construction or complex verification

**Lessons for EyeOnSite:**
- Low pay = low quality. We must pay inspectors well ($10–$80 range, not $5–$20).
- Drive-by verification ≠ construction progress monitoring. Our tasks require more skill.
- 30K agents at $5-$20/task proves supply scales even at low pay.

---

### ProxyPics

**What they do:** Property photography platform for real estate appraisers, lenders, and insurers. 180,000+ data collectors. Advanced capabilities: LiDAR, 3D, 360° photography.

**Markets:** US

**Model:** Marketplace. Clients order property photos. Collectors travel to site and deliver. Pricing: $22–$125/task.

**Strengths:**
- Massive network (180K+ collectors)
- Advanced tech (LiDAR, 3D — future of property data)
- Strong lender/insurer client base
- Higher pay per task than iVueit

**Weaknesses:**
- US only
- Photography-focused (no structured assessment)
- No construction monitoring vertical
- No escrow (enterprise billing)

**Lessons for EyeOnSite:**
- $22–$125/task pricing validates our $20–$200 range
- 180K collectors proves network effects at scale
- LiDAR/3D is future — add to V4+ roadmap (drone integration)
- Photography alone isn't enough — our value is structured assessment + escrow

## Tier 3: Adjacent Services (Diaspora Finance)

### NALA ($40M Series A, 2024)

**What they do:** Money transfer for African diaspora. UK/EU → Africa. Mobile-first. Low fees.

**Relevance to EyeOnSite:** NALA solves money movement. EyeOnSite solves trust after money lands. Partnership opportunity: "Send money with NALA, verify with EyeOnSite." NALA users are our exact target customer.

**Partnership risk:** NALA could build verification themselves. Unlikely — it's outside their core competency (payments infrastructure vs. physical operations). More likely to partner than build.

### LemFi ($1B+/month transaction volume)

**What they do:** Cross-border payments for African and Caribbean diaspora. High volume.

**Relevance:** Same as NALA. Partnership play. LemFi users send money for construction/property — they need verification.

### Flutterwave

**What they do:** Payment infrastructure for Africa. We use them for inspector payouts.

**Relevance:** Technology partner (payout rails). Not a competitor. But Flutterwave Marketplace could theoretically build verification features for merchants using their platform.
